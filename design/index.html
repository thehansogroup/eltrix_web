Three directions, same content

GOAL.md §5.2 asks for three genuinely different designs built as real pages, compared, and then one picked — so the choice is between viable options rather than between a pretty one and a usable one. All three carry identical content and meet the accessibility bar below. The pick is yours; the losers get deleted rather than kept as dead alternates.

Direction A

The record

The page is a signed document. Every capability is a line item and every line item carries its evidence directly beneath it — you cannot read a claim here without seeing what backs it.

The risk: it ends in a signing block and opens with a digest rather than a promise. It reads as a document, not a pitch.

Direction B

The graph

A Matrix room is a directed acyclic graph of signed events, so the hero is that graph, drawn in two inks as a pen plotter would draw it. The capabilities hang off it as branches.

The risk: the first thing a visitor meets is a technical drawing that animates itself. It assumes the reader is interested in how Matrix actually works.

Direction C

The panel

This is equipment you run, not a product you buy. A control surface in warm metal and bakelite, with a bank of indicator lamps wired to the same status file the build gates on: lit, half-lit, dark.

The risk: skeuomorphism. It commits to a physical instrument, which is either characterful or dated depending on your taste — which is exactly why it is one of three rather than the only one.

Verified on all three, measured rather than eyeballed

  • WCAG AA on every text/background pair, computed in the browser at both colour schemes — not judged by eye
  • No horizontal overflow at 320px
  • Zero external requests: no CDN fonts, scripts or styles
  • prefers-reduced-motion respected; direction B is the only one with motion at all, and it stops there
  • Visible keyboard focus, landmarks, and lamp states that never rely on colour alone
